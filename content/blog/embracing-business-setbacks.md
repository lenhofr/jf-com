# Embracing Setbacks in Business: Turning Failure Into Better Decisions

**Embracing setbacks in business means examining what happened, accepting responsibility where appropriate, and using the experience to improve your next decision.** For entrepreneurs and corporate leaders, that process can strengthen negotiations, clarify priorities, and reveal weaknesses before they become larger problems.

In my [CanvasRebel Interview](https://canvasrebel.com/meet-jesse-foreman/), I explained:

> “So many successful people have written and spoke on the importance of embracing failure, so it’s something I’ve always prepared for.”

Preparing for failure does not make it painless. You can understand that setbacks are part of pursuing meaningful goals and still feel disappointed when something you worked toward does not happen.

I have experienced that personally. As an attorney and NFL agent, I also work in environments where preparation and commitment cannot guarantee the outcome. Those experiences inform how I think about risk, opportunity, and the decisions that follow an unexpected result.

Publicly pursuing a goal means accepting the possibility of publicly falling short. When that happens, you face a decision about how much authority to give that outcome over your future. I can acknowledge that my campaign failed to achieve its objective while still examining what the experience can teach me. The useful questions are specific. What could I communicate more effectively? Which assumptions deserved closer scrutiny? Where should I direct my time and energy next?

Entrepreneurs face similar questions after an unsuccessful launch, a rejected proposal, or a deal that falls apart. Attention does not guarantee support. Effort does not guarantee the result you want. A strategy needs honest evaluation even when you care deeply about the mission behind it. For a business owner, that evaluation might reveal that customers did not understand the offer, the timing was wrong, or the company committed resources before testing demand. Each explanation calls for a different response. The lesson becomes valuable when it changes a decision.

For entrepreneurs and executives, the parallel is uncertainty. A major customer may change direction. A funding source may disappear. A counterparty may decide that a transaction no longer fits its priorities. In those moments, effective representation requires a clear understanding of the changed circumstances and the options still available. You reassess your position, communicate accurately, and prepare for the next decision without promising an outcome you cannot control.



## How Entrepreneurs Can Learn From Business Setbacks

A disappointing result becomes useful information only when you examine it carefully. Here are five practical steps.

### 1. Describe what happened precisely

“The business is failing” is too broad to guide a response.

“We lost a customer responsible for a substantial portion of revenue” identifies a problem you can investigate. So does “Our proposal did not explain why the additional cost was justified.”

Specific facts make it easier to choose a proportionate response.

### 2. Separate controllable decisions from outside events

Ask what you could reasonably have influenced.

Was the problem pricing, communication, execution, or an assumption that went untested? Did an outside event change otherwise reasonable expectations?

Take responsibility for decisions you can improve. Be equally careful about blaming yourself or your team for circumstances beyond your control. An accurate diagnosis matters more than finding someone to blame.

### 3. Examine the agreements behind the problem

Business setbacks often expose questions that deserved attention earlier:

* Were responsibilities and deliverables clear?
* Did the parties understand payment terms and timing?
* Was there an agreed process for handling changes?
* Were decision-making authority and ownership expectations documented?
* Had anyone considered what would happen if the relationship ended?

These questions can help identify where contract review and legal planning belong in the next stage of the business.

### 4. Make a measurable change

“Communicate better” is an intention. Assigning responsibility for a weekly project update is a change.

“Choose better partners” is an aspiration. Establishing a consistent review process before entering the next relationship is a change.

Identify what you will do differently, who will own it, and when you will evaluate whether it worked.

### 5. Decide whether to continue, adjust, or stop

Persistence is valuable when the evidence supports another attempt. Sometimes the better decision is to narrow the project, renegotiate the arrangement, or leave an opportunity behind.

Ask: **Knowing what I know today, would I make this commitment again?**

The answer can help you distinguish confidence in the opportunity from reluctance to let go of money, time, or pride already invested.

## Why Counseling Belongs in Business Planning

For entrepreneurs and corporate leaders, legal planning is part of making informed decisions. A useful conversation with counsel goes beyond whether an agreement is ready to sign. It examines what the business is trying to accomplish, which assumptions the arrangement depends on, and how the parties expect to respond if circumstances change.

That conversation may involve business formation, ownership arrangements, commercial contracts, negotiations, or an emerging dispute. The value lies in connecting the legal questions to the company’s actual operations and priorities. For businesses working through those issues before the next major commitment can bring greater clarity to both the opportunity and the exposure.

My experiences in law, professional sports, and a political campaign reinforce the importance of that approach. People make consequential decisions with incomplete information. They need room to ask difficult questions, assess uncertainty, and revise a plan when the facts change.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Business Setbacks

### Why is embracing set backs important for entrepreneurs?

Examining set backs can reveal weaknesses in assumptions, processes, and execution. The benefit comes from applying those lessons to future decisions, rather than simply repeating the same approach with renewed enthusiasm.

### What should a business owner do after a setback?

Establish the facts, assess the immediate operational and financial effects, and identify any agreements or obligations that need professional review. Then choose a specific response and set a date to evaluate progress.

### Does resilience mean continuing at any cost?

No. Resilience includes the ability to change direction. Continuing, renegotiating, reducing the scope of a project, or ending it may each be reasonable depending on the evidence and the business’s priorities.

### When should an entrepreneur speak with a business attorney?

Consider involving an attorney before making a significant contractual or ownership commitment, and when a setback raises questions about an existing agreement or dispute. Early discussion gives counsel more opportunity to understand the situation and evaluate available options.
